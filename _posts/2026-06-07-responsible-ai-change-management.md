Responsible AI is often framed as a governance challenge: principles, policies, review boards, model risk controls, and documentation. These elements matter, but they do not create adoption by themselves. The harder work is turning governance into day-to-day leadership behavior.

For executive teams, the practical question is not simply "Do we have an AI policy?" It is "Can our people make better decisions with AI while understanding accountability, risk, and customer impact?"

## Where Change Management Matters

Responsible AI requires new habits across strategy, technology, legal, risk, commercial, medical, human resources, and frontline teams. Leaders need a common language for use cases, risk levels, decision rights, escalation paths, and human oversight.

Change management makes this operating model real. It clarifies who sponsors the change, what behaviors need to shift, how teams learn, and how adoption is measured.

## The Leadership Agenda

Executives should focus on four questions:

- Which AI decisions require human judgment and explicit accountability?
- Which roles need new skills, tools, or incentives?
- How will governance reviews fit the pace of business?
- What evidence will show that AI is improving outcomes responsibly?

The organizations that succeed with AI will not be those with the longest policy documents. They will be the ones that build trust, judgment, and repeatable execution into the way work actually happens.
